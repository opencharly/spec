# 0.2026281.830 — docs(container): state the positive-control leak rate as the measured interval, not a constant

## Summary

Closes opencharly/spec#41 — a **comment-accuracy fix, not a behaviour fix**, in
`container/stream_load_cleanup_test.go`.

The comment guarding this battery recorded its positive control as a constant — *"leaks only ~1
run in 5"* — and derived *"a 3-run battery misses a real leak 51% of the time"* from it. That rate does
not reproduce: re-measured on three trees the control leaked **18/25 (≈0.72)** — 5/5 on `b39ae868`,
7/10 on `40a4541c`, 6/10 on `f1c08774` — and the 95% Clopper-Pearson interval for the recorded 1-in-5
(`[0.005, 0.72]`) meets that only at its extreme edge. A reader who perturbs this test therefore gets
numbers that contradict the note, and the natural next step is to distrust the whole note — including
the part that matters.

So this keeps the fact the comment exists to preserve and drops what does not hold:

- **kept** — the **intermittency** (invisible in the source, and exactly what makes an underpowered
  battery look clean), the **load-bearing** group kill ("removing it alone reintroduces the leak"), and
  **`>=10`**, which survives every measurement taken;
- **dropped** — the constant rate and the `51%` derived from it. That percentage was the reason the old
  text called a 3-run battery "close to useless", and it is the part the numbers do not support: at the
  measured low end a 3-run battery misses a real leak **~6%** of the time (`0.4³`) and at the pooled rate
  **~2%** (`0.28³`). The battery still has to be long — the *low end* governs and the leak is
  intermittent, so a clean `0,0,0` is weak evidence — but the claim now rests on the measured range and
  shows its arithmetic instead of inheriting a number from the constant that did not reproduce;
- **added** — the observed range taken from the cited table itself (**0.6 to 1.0**: `5/5`, `7/10`, `6/10`,
  pooled `18/25 ≈ 0.72`), and the fact that the once-recorded `~1 run in 5` is the one value it does *not*
  take. The **driver of that spread is not established**, and rather than answer an anomaly with
  "environmental / probably" (B14), it is routed to a **named row**: **spec#212**, which owns measuring it
  — quiet vs loaded on one tree first, then core count and scheduler — and will replace the interval with
  a cause plus its measured range.
